02 / Product

Electricity needs the right pressure.

The water analogy is imperfect, but useful: voltage behaves like pressure and current like flow.

Where the analogy breaks: electricity is not water. The mapping only explains why different parts of a network need different voltage levels.
03 / Duty cycle

Renewables make the ride rougher.

Solar and wind fluctuate. Inverters add distortions. Local heat, humidity and salt change the engineering problem.

Article example: a Rajasthan solar farm may face 48°C ambient heat; coastal Tamil Nadu adds humidity and salt spray.

04 / Moat claim

The parts are purchasable. Integration is not.

The essay locates value in making every component work together under a project's exact losses, heat and reliability requirements.

This is the author's moat hypothesis. Evidence to seek: repeat orders, warranty failures, loss penalties, qualification time and customer retention.

08 / Gotchas

A clean story is not finished proof.

The article supplies a hypothesis. It does not remove cycle, execution, governance or valuation risk.

Margins can normalise.

Scarcity, exports, utilisation and raw-material conditions can flatter current economics.

New capacity can dilute returns.

Nearly doubled capacity must fill quickly and earn attractive incremental ROCE.

A moat needs behaviour.

Approvals matter only if they produce repeat orders, low failures and sustained pricing.

Management claims need primary evidence.

Selected quotations and author interpretation are not a substitute for filings and delivery history.

Price expires.

The stated P/E of 23.6 was a snapshot from 8 February 2026.

Unreconciled source figures: the headline says recent ROCE/ROE exceeded 50%; an embedded comparison shows 35–45%+ ROCE. The source does not explain the period or normalisation difference.
09 / Use it

Turn the story into a checklist.

Investigate the two engines. Then write down what would break them.

Segment truthVerify renewables, exports, customer mix and concentration from current filings.
Moat evidenceCheck approvals, field history, failures, guarantees, repeat orders and qualification times.
Cash clocksMeasure receivable days, inventory days, WIP and cash conversion—do not infer them from mix alone.
Expansion returnTrack utilisation, funding and incremental ROCE after new capacity begins producing.
Current priceRecompute valuation using today's filings and market price.
Thesis breakersName the margin, execution, debt, customer and capital-turn events that would disconfirm the thesis.
High ROCE needs a valuable product and fast-moving capital. Both must survive verification.
