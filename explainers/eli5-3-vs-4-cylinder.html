Three cylinders vs four, and why one grunts at idle

How a car engine turns small explosions into spin — and what changes when you split the same engine into 3 bangs instead of 4.

3-cylinder 4-cylinder burning fuel / pushing fresh air + fuel exhaust gas

01 · The job

A push in a straight line has to become spin

Burning fuel pushes a piston down. Wheels need to turn. Everything in an engine exists to bridge that gap.

02 · The analogy

You already know this machine: a bicycle

Your leg pushes in a straight line. The pedal crank turns that into spin. Same colours = same job.

Where the analogy breaks: a cyclist pushes on every half-turn. A cylinder pushes on only one stroke out of four, once every two turns. That gap is the whole 3-vs-4 story.

03 · Parts

Inside one cylinder

A sealed tube, a plug that sits in it like a sliding lid (the piston), two doors at the top (valves), and a spark.

04 · The cycle

Suck, squeeze, bang, blow

Four up-or-down trips of the piston. Only bang actually pushes. The other three are paid for by momentum.

Each trip is half a crank turn, so one full cycle = 2 turns (720°). Every cylinder fires once per 2 turns, no matter how many cylinders there are.

05 · Filling the gaps

More cylinders = fewer silent moments

One power stroke lasts about 180° of crank rotation. Line them up over one full 720° cycle:

3 cylinders: a push every 240°, each lasting ~180° → a 60° gap after every push, with nobody pushing and the next cylinder busy squeezing (which resists).
4 cylinders: a push every 180° → pushes hand off end-to-end. No gap.

06 · The crankshaft

How the crank is shaped decides the rhythm

Looking down the crankshaft from the front, here's where each cylinder's rod attaches.

3-cylinder · pins 120° apart

4-cylinder · pins in pairs, 180° apart

720° ÷ 3 = 240°. 720° ÷ 4 = 180°. The 4-cylinder's outer pair (1 & 4) and inner pair (2 & 3) always move as mirror images. Remember that, it matters in step 09.

08 · The torque pulse

Three big kicks with holes vs four smaller overlapping ones

Twisting force on the crankshaft over one full cycle. Same engine size, same average.

The crankshaft speeds up on every kick and slows in every hole. Newton's third law: the engine block twists the opposite way on its rubber mounts each time. Bigger kicks, deeper holes → more twisting of the block. That rolling, felt through the mounts, is a big part of the 3-cylinder idle shudder.

09 · Balance

The see-saw a 4-cylinder doesn't have

Pistons are heavy and slam to a stop at the top and bottom of every stroke, tugging the block each time. Arrows = that tug, one moment in time.

3-cylinder: forces cancel, but not evenly placed

4-cylinder: mirror image, nothing to rock

Engineers call the 3-cylinder's see-saw a rocking couple. It happens once per crank turn: 13 times a second at an 800 rpm idle.

10 · Why idle, specifically

At idle, the pulses are slow enough to feel

Pulses per second = rpm ÷ 60 × cylinders ÷ 2. At idle the 3-cylinder's rhythm sits low, where cars and bodies shake.

The flywheel is weakest at idle

Each bang is bigger

Put together: bigger kicks + longer holes + see-saw rocking + slow, low rhythm + weak flywheel. That's the idle grunt. Rev it to 3000 and the same pulses come 75 times a second and blur into a hum.

11 · The sound

Why it sounds off-beat

Ticks = each crank turn. Dots = each bang. Over two turns:

The 4-cylinder bangs exactly twice per turn: tidy, even, a buzz. The 3-cylinder bangs 1.5 times per turn, so the bangs fall on and off the crank's own once-per-turn rocking. That syncopation, at a low pitch, is the thrummy "half a six-cylinder" growl people either love or hate.

12 · The gotcha

The 4-cylinder isn't perfect either

Because the rod tilts, a piston covers more distance in the top half of the crank's turn than the bottom. It moves faster up there, so its tug is stronger at the top.

Uneven halves

Score sheet

In a 4-cylinder, all four pistons hit that "stronger at the top" moment together, so the extra tugs add up: a vertical buzz at twice engine speed that grows with rpm². That's why a 4-cylinder gets coarse near the redline, and why big ones (2.0 L+) often carry balance shafts. In a 3-cylinder those tugs cancel. Each layout trades one vibration for another.

14 · So what

Why anyone builds a 3-cylinder

Fewer cylinders means fewer moving parts rubbing, and less cylinder wall to leak heat into. You pay for that in smoothness at low rpm.

You'll find 3-cylinders in

Maruti Suzuki K10 & the newer Swift's Z12E · Tata Tiago/Altroz 1.2 · Hyundai 1.0 turbo · Ford 1.0 EcoBoost · BMW/Mini 1.5 · Toyota GR Yaris 1.6

What to expect from one

A noticeable thrum at idle and when lugging at very low rpm in a high gear. Once above ~1500–2000 rpm, most feel and sound perfectly normal. Keep revs up a little and it rewards you.

AN IDEA TO TAKE WITH YOU
A 3-cylinder is not a worse 4-cylinder. It is a different rhythm: fewer, bigger, further-apart bangs, and a block that rocks instead of buzzing.
