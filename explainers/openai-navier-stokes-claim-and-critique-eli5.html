Navier–Stokes, without the fog

What OpenAI says it solved

It did not prove that ordinary water suddenly goes infinite. It says it built one carefully pushed vortex that makes the fluid equations break.

The key twist: that outside push makes the result less natural—but the official prize wording explicitly allows it.

01 · The problem

Can the fluid rulebook break?

The equations predict how liquid or gas moves. The open question is whether a smooth flow always stays mathematically sensible—or can demand infinite speed after a finite time.

02 · Three ways to hear it

The headline hides the exact claim

Switch lenses. The argument changes once “solved Navier–Stokes” is replaced with the actual theorem and the actual prize wording.

What people hear

“AI solved how fluids behave.”

That sounds broad: normal fluid, no special help, mystery finished. It is not what OpenAI’s theorem says.

06 · The technical heart

The push cannot cheat

It is easy to imagine an exploding flow if the outside push also explodes. Clay does not allow that. OpenAI says its force stays smooth.

Not allowed

Hide the explosion inside the push

That would merely command the answer and would fail the smooth-force condition.

OpenAI’s claim

Let tiny internal waves do the cancelling

The paper adds small, fast flow patterns whose momentum cancels the bad leftovers around the main vortex.

07 · Scope

What the claim says—and doesn’t

The cleanest way to avoid hype is to keep the narrow theorem beside the conclusions it cannot support.

OpenAI does claim

✓One fluid starts smoothly—from rest.
✓A finite, smooth outside push is applied.
✓Peak speed becomes unlimited in finite time.
✓Total energy stays bounded.
✓The proof has a public Lean version.

It does not yet establish

×Normal water breaks without a designed push.
×Most fluid flows fail.
×Real liquid literally reaches infinite speed.
×Independent mathematicians broadly accept it.
×Lean alone settles whether the encoded claim is the right one.

09 · The fair verdict

Both sides are talking past each other

The formal question and the physically natural question are related, but they are not identical.

Where OpenAI has a point

The forced route is officially allowed.

Clay’s C and D options explicitly permit a smooth outside force. If the proof is correct and the formal version matches it, this is a serious answer to the written problem.

Where Mehdi has a point

The headline sounds broader than the result.

The theorem does not settle the unforced case people usually picture. Independent review, physical meaning, attribution, and what the AI actually understood remain separate questions.

Bottom line

OpenAI claims one carefully pushed vortex breaks the equations in an officially permitted way. Mehdi objects to treating that narrow, engineered, not-yet-broadly-accepted result as the final conquest of the fluid mystery.
