Fundamental analysis in one page

A share is a slice of a business. Its price wiggles every day; what the slice is worth follows what the business earns over years. Fundamental analysis is the habit of asking what a company earns and what that is worth, before asking what the screen says.

The building block

A share is a slice of a business

Ramu Chai Ltd has 10 crore shares example. Own 1 crore of them and you own 10% of the company, including 10% of its profit. The year’s profit is ₹50 crore example, so each crore of shares stands behind ₹5 crore of it.

The market’s number

Market cap is price times shares

Multiply the price of one slice by the number of slices and you get market cap: what the market currently charges for the whole company. ₹100 × 10 crore = ₹1,000 crore example. Double the shares and halve the price, and nothing about the business changed: same area, same market cap. Part 21 covers splits and dilution.

The catch

The price moves; the business doesn’t

One week at Ramu Chai example: the price closes at ₹96, ₹103, ₹99, ₹107 and ₹100, so market cap swings between ₹960 crore and ₹1,070 crore. The tea sold, the costs paid and the profit earned that week were the same. The price moved because people’s moods, news and flows of money moved.

The other number

Value starts with what is left over

Ramu Chai sold ₹500 crore of tea example. After costs and tax (₹440 crore, net of ₹4 crore of interest earned) and ₹10 crore of interest on its borrowings, ₹50 crore was left for the owners example. That profit is what a share’s value ultimately rests on. Part 3 walks down this ladder line by line.

Go deeper: where the ₹440 crore comes from

Cost of materials 250, employee benefits 60, other expenses 105, depreciation 12 and tax 17 add up to 444. Other income of 4 (interest on cash) is netted off, giving 440 example. Sales 500 − 440 − interest 10 = profit 50.

Go deeper: exactly what the lab computes

The price path is a fixed random series, not real market data example: each five-minute step nudges the price’s distance from value by a small random amount (about 1.2% a day), and a pull back towards value that halves a gap in about a year. Value per share is ₹1,000 crore ÷ shares, growing at the profit-growth rate. Today’s price is exactly your slider. Market cap = price × shares. Real prices have jumps, trends and crashes that this toy does not model.

Two ways to look

Reading the price, or the business

Technical analysis studies the price and volume history itself, its patterns and trends, mostly to time trades. Fundamental analysis studies the company behind the price: its accounts, its business and what it is worth, mostly to decide what to pay. Both are used in practice; this series teaches the second. The lab shows why it is a sensible place to start: over a day the price is mostly noise, over years it follows the business.

So what

Next: where the numbers live

From now on, a price is the start of a question, not the answer. Three to ask of any listed company:

  1. What does the business earn, and is that growing?
  2. What are the shares, together, being valued at (market cap), and how does that compare with the earnings?
  3. Did the price move because the business changed, or because the mood did?

To answer them you need the company’s accounts. Part 2, Where the numbers live, shows where they sit: the annual report, the exchange filings, standalone versus consolidated, and the quarterly results.

AN IDEA TO TAKE WITH YOU
The price is what the market pays today, the value is what the business earns over years, and fundamental analysis is learning to tell them apart.
