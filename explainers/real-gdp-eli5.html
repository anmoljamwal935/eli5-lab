How India turns rupees into real GDP

There is no single "inflation number" subtracted from GDP. Each sector is cleaned of its own price changes, then the sectors are added up.

The claim

"Real GDP = nominal − imports − deflator"

That formula was offered in a televised debate. Both subtractions are wrong, and the formula hides how real GDP is actually built.

The problem

You can't add kilometres to litres

GDP is the value of final goods and services produced inside India in a period (equivalently, the sum of every producer's value added). Roads, milk and haircuts have no common unit, so each is valued in rupees first. Only then can they be added.

The catch

₹100 → ₹110 could mean two opposite things

Rupee GDP that still contains price changes is nominal. Remove the price effect and you get real GDP, the part that is actual extra production.

The building block

Count only what each firm adds

The video's example: Keshav Enterprises buys goods for ₹40 and sells for ₹44. Only the ₹4 it adds counts. Add every firm's slice and you get gross value added (GVA). We reuse this firm for the rest of the page.

The textbook shortcut

Divide everything by headline CPI? No.

Headline CPI is the inflation number in the news: price changes of 300+ household items, averaged by weight. Schools imply GDP is deflated by it. But it tracks what households buy, not what each sector produces.

What MoSPI actually does

Each sector gets its own price lens

The statistics office splits the economy into sectors and removes each one's own price changes, using 300+ item-level indices. Then it adds up the real values. Headline CPI is only a fallback where no better index exists.

The mechanism

Single vs double deflation

An oil shock lifts Keshav's input prices 6%. Competition lets him raise his own prices only 3%. He makes exactly as much as before. The old method ran inputs and output through one index. The new one gives each its own.

"Double" means two separate deflations, one for inputs and one for output. The IMF's 2017 national-accounts manual recommends it. India's new 2022-23 series dropped single deflation entirely, using double deflation in manufacturing and agriculture (the video says manufacturing only).

The surprise

Every price rose, yet the deflator fell

The deflator is nominal ÷ real. Keshav's nominal value added shrank to ₹2.92 while real stayed ₹4, so his deflator drops to 73. The video calls this ulti ganga, a river running backwards. It isn't a claim that prices fell.

That's what happened in the new series: manufacturing's deflator grew −1.5% (figure cited in the video). People read it as "the government says factory prices fell." A falling deflator alone doesn't say why. The video's explanation for this release is the Keshav pattern: input costs rose faster than factories could pass on, which is what a global cost shock looks like.

Our toy firm's drop is extreme because ₹4 is a thin slice of ₹44. Thin margins magnify price moves, and any error in the price indices, many times over.

The common mistake

A method, a ratio, and an inflation rate

The debate's punchline: "double deflation's value is 2.3, but inflation is 4.9." That mixes three different kinds of thing.

The deflator and CPI price different baskets, and India's deflator nets out input costs. A gap between 2.3% and 4.9% is expected when input prices jump. It doesn't by itself prove the GDP numbers are wrong or right.

So what

Three questions before trusting a GDP take

  • Which sector? Services, manufacturing and agriculture are each deflated differently.
  • Which price index? Headline CPI is the households' basket, not the economy's.
  • Method or number? "Double deflation" is a technique. The deflator is a ratio. CPI is an index; its % change is the inflation rate.

Source for the method: MoSPI GDP FAQ, Q14–15 and Q25 ↗. A sound method still depends on good data and gets revised.

AN IDEA TO TAKE WITH YOU
Real GDP isn't nominal GDP minus "inflation". It's every sector cleaned of its own price changes, then added up.
