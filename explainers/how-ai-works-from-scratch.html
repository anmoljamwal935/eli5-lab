AI & FRONTIER / SERIES MAPELI5

How AI works, from scratch

A series of studies that starts with no background at all and ends with a full picture of how an AI chatbot works: the program that writes the answers, and the chips it runs on. Each part builds on the one before, so you can read them in order and never meet a word you have not seen drawn first.

The plan

Two tracks, one machine

An AI chatbot is two things working together. The LLM (large language model) is the program: a huge pile of numbers that turns your message into an answer. The chips are the hardware that does the arithmetic those numbers need. The series follows both, and switches between them whenever one side needs the other to make sense.

Read along the line from left to right. A filled dot is published; a hollow one is coming.

How to read it

The same colours mean the same things

Every diagram in every part uses one colour key, so after a part or two you can read the drawings without a legend.

Text and tokensWhat people type and read: letters, words, and the pieces the model cuts them into.
Stored numbersNumbers the model keeps: what it learned in training, and what it remembers about a conversation.
ArithmeticNumbers being worked out right now: scores, sums, multiplications.
ConnectionsThings linking to other things: words looking at words, wires between chips.
The wrong intuitionWhat most people guess, shown so you can see exactly where it goes wrong.

Facts carry a tag. public means a published paper, spec sheet or plain arithmetic. example means a made-up but consistent number used to show how something works. not public means the company has not said. Each part also has Go deeper boxes. They are optional: skip them on a first read and nothing later breaks.

    Training a model (part 6) is just an enormous amount of multiplying, which only makes sense once you know why a graphics card is good at multiplying (part 5). Why a model is slow to answer long questions (part 14) comes down to memory (part 8). So the order follows the questions you would naturally ask, not one subject at a time.

    The bonus study, Inside LLM serving, is where the two tracks meet: one model on a group of chips answering millions of people at once. It is written to be read last, when every word in it is familiar.

    AN IDEA TO TAKE WITH YOU
    An AI chatbot is a very large pile of numbers, and a very fast machine for multiplying them. Everything else is detail.
