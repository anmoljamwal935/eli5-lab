GDP: Separate the Bread from the Prices

Real GDP measures production without mistaking higher prices for more output.

Built from Keshav Bedi’s video (Hindi captions), with mechanics checked against MoSPI’s FAQ; toy numbers, not reported GDP estimates · Watch the source ↗

The analogy

A bakery is a tiny economy

Flour becomes bread. GDP counts the new value producers create inside a country—not every rupee that changes hands. Teal means inputs; coral means output and value added.

The running example

Sales are not all new value

Our toy bakery sells 100 loaves for ₹1,000. Purchased inputs cost ₹600. Gross value added (GVA) is ₹400. We reuse this bakery throughout.

The problem

Bigger receipts, same bread

Keep quantities unchanged. If both prices rise 10%, nominal GVA becomes ₹440. At old prices it is still ₹400. Real growth is zero.

The mapping

Remove each price change separately

Now produce 105 loaves using 5% more inputs. Bread prices rise 10%; input prices rise 20%. Adjust each bill separately. That is double deflation.

Real GVA = output ÷ output-price factor − inputs ÷ input-price factor. “Double” means two separate adjustments, not subtracting inflation twice.

The mechanism

Subtract after removing prices

At base-year prices, ₹1,050 of output minus ₹630 of inputs leaves ₹420. Compared with ₹400, real value added has grown 5%.

Try it

Change prices, not production

Output and input quantities stay 5% higher. True GVA growth stays +5%. Change prices and watch two shortcuts drift away from that truth.

The common mistake

A falling deflator needn’t mean cheaper bread

Our price spike leaves nominal GVA at ₹399 but real GVA at ₹420. The GVA deflator is 95. Bread is dearer; input costs squeezed the margin.

Implicit deflator = nominal ÷ real × 100. A fall alone does not identify its cause. Thin value-added margins also magnify input- and output-price measurement errors.

Field guide

Which inflation do you mean?

Each index follows a particular basket. Match that basket to the question; household inflation is not a universal adjustment for production prices.

Headline CPI

Households’ weighted consumption basket.

Consumer inflation.

Item-level CPI

A component, such as a service category.

Useful when it matches the activity.

WPI / PPI

Wholesale / producer-price measures.

Relevant goods and production prices.

GDP deflator

Nominal GDP ÷ real GDP × 100.

Implied price measure for domestic output.

The whole economy

Add domestic value, not every sale

Add all GVA, then product taxes minus subsidies: GDP. Imports are removed in the spending identity to exclude foreign production—not to turn nominal GDP into real GDP.

So what

Read India’s GDP debate better

Ask which sector, which index, and which comparable series. A sound method does not guarantee perfect data. A CPI–deflator gap alone proves neither fraud nor accuracy.

India’s 2022–23-base series uses double deflation in manufacturing and agriculture—not manufacturing alone—with sector-specific methods elsewhere. MoSPI FAQ, questions 9 and 14–15 ↗.

AN IDEA TO TAKE WITH YOU
Remove the right price changes from output and inputs separately; then measure the value actually added.
