How a lithium-ion battery works

A rechargeable chemical pump: it shuttles lithium ions around inside so electrons march around outside.

electrons — travel the outside wire
lithium ions — travel inside the cell

Two kinds of traffic, two separate roads. Blue electrons can only go around the outside, through your gadget. Green ions can only move inside. The trick of the battery is linking them.

01 · The problem

No outlet, no power?

Plugged in: alive

The wall pushes an endless stream of electrons through the phone. But the phone is chained to the wall.

Cut loose: dead

Pull the plug and the push disappears. A battery is bottled push — chemistry that travels with you.

Gadgets run on flowing electrons (current). The wall socket is an endless but immobile pump. A battery packs a smaller pump into your pocket.

02 · The analogy

Two tanks and a wheel

Water falls from the high tank, spins the wheel, collects below. The pump lifts it back up to run again. A battery does exactly this — with ions instead of water.

04 · The mechanism

Inside the cell: two roads

lithium ions slip through the separator
electrons are blocked — they must go around

Five thin layers, thinner than paper. The separator is the bouncer: tiny ions pass, electrons don't. That forced detour is the electric current.

05 · The mechanism, live

One loop, two directions

3.7 V · 62%

Discharging, ions drift right inside while electrons loop around outside. Charging reverses the whole parade. The slider is the battery meter: ions left means full.

06 · Going deeper

Why lithium, why 4 volts

Lithium ions are the tiniest metal ions, so they slide between graphite sheets like letters into files. And the voltage number is just the chemistry's eagerness gap — about 4 volts when full.

07 · The gotchas

Three traps for beginners

myth

Electrons never cross inside

If they could, the cell would short itself and die in minutes. The separator blocks them completely.

myth

Current arrows point backwards

Engineers draw current + to −, opposite the electrons. It predates the electron's discovery — we're stuck with it.

limit

Every cycle wears it out

Crust grows, cathodes crack, lithium gets trapped. That's why phones die at 80% health — and why cooling matters.

Not trivia: these three explain why batteries need management chips, cooling, and gentle charging. Respect the chemistry and it lasts years.

08 · So what

From earbuds to power grids

One cell is whisper-quiet chemistry. Stack thousands and you get cars; millions, and solar power works after dark. Rechargeable lithium made portable energy ordinary.
