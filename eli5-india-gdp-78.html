1 · The problem

One number, two opposite stories

For April–June, GDP grew 7.8% over the same quarter last year — faster than the RBI expected. Half the internet celebrated. The other half said: look at jobs and wages first.

7.8%

The headline

Fastest-growing large economy. Beat forecasts. Bugles out, kilts flowing.

Hmm…

The street

Stagnant pay, thin consumption, high household debt. Growth you can't feel is a rumour.

To judge the fight you need two ideas: how growth is counted (the maths debate) and what growth is worth (the lived-experience debate). This page covers both.

4 · How it works, part 1

They changed the ruler mid-race

In February the base year moved from 2011–12 to 2022–23, and old quarters were redrawn on the new scale. Last year's quarter shrank from ₹86.1 lakh crore (old ruler) to about ₹80 lakh crore (new ruler). Growth must be measured ruler-to-ruler.

The government's reply, fairly stated: the old ₹86 figure belongs to a retired series — comparing it with today's number is a category error, and revisions like this happen in every country. Critics reply: revisions that always flatter the present deserve sunlight, not trust.
5 · How it works, part 2

The eraser looks too small

The official eraser says economy-wide inflation was only ~2.5%. But shoppers felt ~3.9% (CPI) and wholesalers saw 9%+ (WPI). Why? They measure different baskets — and the GDP eraser blends 300+ of them, including calm services that drag the average down.

Try it: you set the eraser

Nominal growth is fixed at 10.3%. Drag the eraser and watch real growth change. Small eraser, big headline.

Why economists grumble: with near-zero wholesale inflation that quarter, the eraser shrank and real growth ballooned — possibly flattered by ~1 point. A low eraser today also quietly raises the bar for every quarter after it.
6 · How it works, part 3

The bakery that broke the eraser

Manufacturing grew 7.7% in rupees but 9.2% "real" — meaning its eraser was negative (−1.5%). How can inflation be below zero when nothing got cheaper? New method: erase the flour and the bread separately.

Not magic, just new accounting: when input prices outrun output prices, real filling outgrows the nominal loaf. Sensible in a war-shocked quarter — but brand-new machinery, so veterans want it audited, not applauded.
7 · The gotcha

A first sketch, not the painting

Quarterly GDP is an early estimate built from partial data. It gets revised five or six times; this quarter's "final" arrives around February 2029 — 30 months away. Arguing over the second decimal today is arguing over wet paint.

Two honest footnotes: the "discrepancy" line critics wave around is just a balancing item between two counting methods — it shrinks to ~zero by the final estimate. And this base revision actually shrank nominal GDP (India slipped a rank) — not the move of a rigged scale.
8 · So what

One number is not the economy

Between 2004–08, 8% growth could be felt on the street. Today's version can't be — which is exactly why the critics say: don't stare at the scorecard, watch the classroom.

"When a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good measure."

— Goodhart's Law. The GDP figure now travels on WhatsApp as proof, not as information. Treat it as a clue; confirm with jobs, wages and shop sales.
